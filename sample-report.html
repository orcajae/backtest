Sample report · Quick backtest + stress test · prepared by Jay Kim

200-day average on SPY

Daily data, 3 January 2000 to 13 November 2026. Prices adjusted for dividends and splits (Yahoo Finance). This is a public textbook rule, used here to show what a report contains. It is not a recommendation.

The rules as tested

BuySPY closes above its 200-day simple average
Sell to cashSPY closes below its 200-day simple average
ExecutionPosition changes from the next day; a signal never trades on its own bar
Costs0.05% of the trade on every buy and every sell
CashEarns nothing while out of the market

Findings

1. It cut the worst fall roughly in half (−28.5% against −55.2%). In 2008 the rule returned −4.1% while buy and hold returned −36.8%.

2. It gave up about 2 points of return a year and beat buy and hold in only 5 of 27 calendar years. It lagged most in fast rebounds: in 2020 it made 9.6% against 18.3%.

3. The result depends on the number 200. At 100 days the protection disappears (worst fall −51.6%). A rule whose edge moves this much with one setting needs a reason for its setting before it is trusted.

4. Signals cluster in sideways markets. In March 2025 it switched three times in 16 days; each switch costs money and the cost compounds.

Limits of this report

  • Past prices only. Nothing here predicts future returns.
  • Cash earns 0%. With interest on cash the rule would look somewhat better.
  • Taxes, slippage beyond the stated cost, and borrowing are not modelled.
  • One market. A rule that works on SPY may not work elsewhere.
